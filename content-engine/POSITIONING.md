# Positioning

## Who you are
8+ years in video production. Own studio (CHiGGY Studio, Jodhpur). Creative Lead
and Senior Creative Head roles. Video editing, videography, direction, photography,
graphic design. You have shipped real work for real clients under real deadlines.

## The position
**"The working creative who tests AI on actual client work."**

Not an AI news account. Not a tech commentator. A practitioner who happens to
report from inside the job.

## Why this wins
The AI-news category is saturated with people re-narrating newsletters. They have
no way to answer the only question your audience actually has:
**"Does this survive contact with a paying client?"**

You can answer it. That is the entire moat.

## The reframe, story by story

| Generic AI account says | You say |
|---|---|
| "Runway launched Gen-5" | "I replaced a day of B-roll with Gen-5 on a real client cut. Here's the shot that broke." |
| "This tool does AI video" | "I gave the same brief to the AI and to my editor. Here's the difference a client would notice." |
| "10 AI tools for creators" | "I put 3 of these into my actual pipeline for a week. Two got deleted." |
| "AI is replacing editors" | "Here's the exact part of my job AI took this year — and the part it can't touch." |

**The rule: every Reel earns credibility from something you personally ran.**

## Content pillars (rotate — don't do the same pillar two days running)

1. **The Test** (~40%) — you ran the new tool on real footage. Verdict + evidence.
   *Highest trust, highest saves. This is your signature format.*
2. **The News, Creative-Lens** (~25%) — a launch, explained only in terms of what it
   changes for people who shoot and edit.
3. **The Workflow** (~20%) — a piece of your actual pipeline. Where AI sits, where it doesn't.
4. **The Honest Take** (~15%) — pricing reality, what's overhyped, what clients
   actually ask for. Contrarian but earned.

## Voice
- **Hinglish is likely your advantage, not a compromise.** Jodhpur + the Indian
  creator market is enormous and far less saturated in this niche than English AI
  content. Technical terms stay in English, the framing goes Hindi. Your call —
  but English-only is fighting the most crowded version of this market.
- Talk like you're showing a junior editor something on your monitor. Not like a
  news anchor.
- Show the screen. You have the footage, the timeline, the studio. Use them —
  talking-head-only content wastes your single biggest unfair advantage.

## What to stop before you start
- Don't do "top 5 AI tools" listicles. Saturated, zero trust, no moat.
- Don't report a launch you haven't opened yourself.
- Don't hide the studio. B-roll of you actually working outperforms any stock visual.
