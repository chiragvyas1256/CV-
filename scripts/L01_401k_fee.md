# L01 – The 1% Fee That Can Cost You $250,000
**Keyword:** 401k fees / expense ratio | **Length:** about 8–9 min | All figures [VERIFY] before recording.

| # | Time | Spoken narration | Visual / B-roll |
|---|------|------------------|-----------------|
| 1 | 0:00 | Two people. Same job, same salary, same 401(k) contributions. Thirty years later, one has $1.31 million. The other has $1.06 million. The difference is a fee you never see on your paycheck. | Split screen, two bank balances ticking up |
| 2 | 0:15 | By the end of this video you'll know where that fee hides, how to find yours in two minutes, and what to do about it. | Title card |
| 3 | 0:40 | First, what the fee is. Every fund in your 401(k) charges an annual fee called an expense ratio. It's a percentage of what you have invested, and it's taken out automatically. You never get a bill. | Screen recording: sample fund page, "Expense ratio" highlighted |
| 4 | 1:30 | A fee of 0.10% means you pay ten cents a year per hundred dollars. A fee of 1% means a dollar. It sounds tiny. It isn't. | Animated coins |
| 5 | 2:15 | Here's why. Fees don't just cost you the fee. They cost you everything that fee would have earned for the next thirty years. It compounds in reverse. | Compounding curve animation |
| 6 | 3:30 | Worked example. You start with $100,000 and add $500 a month. Assume 7% yearly growth before fees. With a 0.10% fee, you end with about $1.31 million. With a 1% fee, about $1.06 million. That gap is roughly $250,000. Same market, same effort. | Animated bar chart, numbers highlighted |
| 7 | 5:00 | Where do high fees hide? Actively managed funds, target-date funds from some providers, and plan-level administration fees on top. Check all three. | Three-icon list |
| 8 | 6:00 | How to find yours. Log in, open your fund list, find the column called expense ratio or net expense. Under 0.20% is excellent. Over 0.75% deserves a question. | Screen recording walkthrough |
| 9 | 7:00 | What to do Monday: one, list your funds' expense ratios. Two, compare to the cheapest index fund in the same plan. Three, switch future contributions if the gap is big. Four, ask HR for the plan's fee disclosure. | On-screen checklist |
| 10 | 8:00 | Next up: the number that quietly decides whether you get approved for a loan, your credit score, and how it's really calculated. | End screen → L02 |

**Sources:** DOL fee disclosure guidance (dol.gov), SEC "Mutual Fund Fees and Expenses" (investor.gov). Math: monthly compounding, 7% gross, fee subtracted from annual return.
